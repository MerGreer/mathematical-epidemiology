Activity 11.6.

In this activity, we solve algebraically for the endemic equilibrium \((\overline{S}, \overline{I}, \overline{R} )\) of the compartmental model shown in Figure 11.3. The lines atop \(S\text{,}\) \(I\text{,}\) and \(R\) indicate that these are the specific values of each compartment’s population for which the entire system is at equilibrium. To help us start the algebra needed to compute the formulas we seek, we use the knowledge that at equilibrium all three differential equations from the model in Activity 11.2 are set equal to 0, indicating that the values are unchanging for all three populations \(S(t)\text{,}\) \(I(t)\text{,}\) and \(R(t)\text{.}\) Here are the expressions that result when we set the derivatives of the model equal to 0:
🔗
\begin{align} \alpha R - \beta SI \amp = 0 \tag{11.2}\\ \beta SI - \gamma I \amp = 0 \tag{11.3}\\ \gamma I - \alpha R \amp = 0. \tag{11.4} \end{align}
🔗
Follow the steps below to determine the combination of algebraic formulas for which (11.2), (11.3), and (11.4) are valid.
🔗
  1. Solve (11.3) for \(S\text{.}\) In other words, rearrange \(\beta SI - \gamma I=0\) into an equation beginning with “\(S=\)” and having only non-\(S\) terms on the right-hand side. Your result can also be written as \(\overline{S}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Start with (11.2) and replace \(R\) with \(N-S-I\text{.}\) Explain why this is a permissible substitution. Then solve for \(I\text{.}\) Your answer will be in terms of \(S\text{,}\) which we solved for in part (1). Your result can also be written as \(\overline{I}\text{,}\) with \(S\) on the right-hand side written as \(\overline{S}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Solve (11.4) for \(R\text{.}\) Your answer will be in terms of \(I\text{,}\) which we solved for in part (2). Your result can also be written as \(\overline{R}\text{,}\) with \(I\) on the right-hand side written as \(\overline{I}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Your result should be three algebraic formulas giving equilibrium values \(\overline{S}\text{,}\) \(\overline{I}\text{,}\) and \(\overline{R}\) for the three populations in the model, entirely in terms of the parameter values \(\alpha\text{,}\) \(\beta\text{,}\) and \(\gamma\text{,}\) as well as the total population size \(N\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
  1. \(\overline{S}=\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\)
    🔗
    Be sure to show your algebra steps.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\overline{I}=\frac{\alpha N - \alpha \overline{S}}{\alpha + \beta \overline{S}}\)
    🔗
    Be sure to show your algebra steps. Then, we could substitute in \(\overline{S}=\gamma / \beta\) to obtain a formula that is entirely in terms of the model’s parameters and the total population value.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(\overline{R}=\frac{\gamma \overline{I}}{\alpha}\)
    🔗
