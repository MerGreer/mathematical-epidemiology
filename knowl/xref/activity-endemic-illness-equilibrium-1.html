Activity 12.6.

In this activity, we solve algebraically for the endemic equilibrium \((\overline{S}, \overline{I}, \overline{R} )\) of the compartmental model shown in Figure 12.3. The lines atop \(S\text{,}\) \(I\text{,}\) and \(R\) indicate that these are the specific values of each compartment’s population for which the entire system is at equilibrium. To help us start the algebra needed to compute the formulas we seek, we use the knowledge that at equilibrium all three differential equations from the model in Activity 12.6 are set equal to 0, indicating that the values are unchanging for all three populations \(S(t)\text{,}\) \(I(t)\text{,}\) and \(R(t)\text{.}\) Follow the steps below to determine the combination of algebraic formulas for which this is true.
🔗
\begin{align} \alpha R - \beta SI \amp = 0 \tag{12.2}\\ \beta SI - \gamma I \amp = 0 \tag{12.3}\\ \gamma I - \alpha R \amp = 0 \tag{12.4} \end{align}
🔗
  1. Solve (12.3) for \(S\text{.}\) In other words, rearrange \(\beta SI - \gamma I=0\) into an equation beginning with “\(S=\)” and having only non-\(S\) terms on the right-hand side.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Start with (12.2) and replace \(R\) with \(N-S-I\text{.}\) Explain why this is a permissible substitution. Then solve for \(I\text{.}\) Your answer will be in terms of \(S\text{,}\) which we solved for in part (1).
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Use the \(dR/dt=0\) equation, that is, \(\gamma I - \alpha R=0\) to solve for \(R\text{.}\) Your answer will be in terms of \(I\text{,}\) which we solved for in part (2).
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Your result should be three algebraic formulas giving equilibrium values for the \(S\text{,}\) \(I\text{,}\) and \(R\) populations, entirely in terms of the parameter values \(\alpha\text{,}\) \(\beta\text{,}\) and \(\gamma\text{,}\) as well as the total population size \(N\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
  1. \(\displaystyle \overline{S}=\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\overline{I}=\frac{\alpha N - \alpha S}{\alpha + \beta S}\)
    🔗
    We could substitute in \(\overline{S}=\gamma / \beta\) to obtain a formula that is entirely in terms of the model’s parameters and the total population value.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(\overline{R}=\frac{\gamma I}{\alpha}\)
    🔗
    We could substitute in the formula for \(\overline{I}\text{,}\) and within that substitute the formula for \(\overline{S}\text{,}\) to obtain a formula that is entirely in terms of the model’s parameters and the total population value.
    🔗
