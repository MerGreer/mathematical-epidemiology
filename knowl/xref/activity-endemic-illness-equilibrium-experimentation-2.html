Answer.
  1. Initial population values N, S_start, R_start, and I_start are defined in lines 18-21. In line 22, the code forms these values into the vector y0 of initial values for the \(S\text{,}\) \(I\text{,}\) and \(R\) populations.
    🔗
    The equilibrium formulas for each population, shown in lines 24-26, are as follows:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \overline{S}=\frac{\mu + \gamma}{\beta} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \overline{I}=\frac{\Pi}{\mu + \gamma}-\frac{\mu}{\beta} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \overline{R}=\frac{\gamma}{\mu}\overline{I}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Be sure to show the algebra steps leading to these equilibrium values.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. By the rules of calculus and derivatives, the sum of derivatives equals the derivative of a sum. That is, we can add the derivatives \(dS/dt\text{,}\) \(dI/dt\text{,}\) and \(dR/dt\text{,}\) or we can first add \(S+I+R\) and then compute the derivative \(d(S+I+R)/dt\text{,}\) and the answers will be the same. Since \(S+I+R=N\text{,}\) then we know that both \(dS/dt+dI/dt+dR/dt\) and \(d(S+I+R)/dt\) equal \(dN/dt\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Next, sum the other sides of the differential equations developed in Activity 11.4:
    \begin{align*} \amp (\Pi-\mu S-\beta SI) \\ + \amp (\beta SI - \mu I - \gamma I) \\ + \amp (\gamma I - \mu R) \end{align*}
    to obtain
    \begin{equation*} \Pi - \mu S - \mu I - \mu R\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Since this result equals \(dN/dt\text{,}\) we set it equal to 0, substituting in \(N=S+I+R\text{.}\) This leads to the result
    \begin{equation*} N=\frac{\Pi}{\mu}\text{.} \end{equation*}
    Therefore, the equilibrium value \(\overline{N}\) is \(\Pi /\mu\text{.}\)
