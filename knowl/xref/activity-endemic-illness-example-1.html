Activity 12.2.

Consider the compartmental diagram shown in Figure 12.3, depicting an illness in which people in the Removed compartment can lose immunity and return to the Susceptible compartment. Use the questions below the diagram to think through the details of this model.
🔗
  1. Write out your interpretation of the \(\alpha R\) formula and its accompanying arrow.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. In what way does this model represent an endemic illness rather than an epidemic?
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Sometimes the R compartment in a model includes only people who have recovered from illness, meaning the R could stand for Recovered. Alternatively, sometimes the R compartment includes recovered people as well as others, such as those who have died, in which case we typically say that R stands for Removed. Write a reasonable interpretation for the R compartment shown in Figure 12.3.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Write out the differential equations for the compartmental model shown in Figure 12.3.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Answer.
  1. The formula \(\alpha R\) describes movement from the R compartment to the S compartment. The value of \(\alpha\) indicates how quickly people move from R to S.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. In this model, people enter the S compartment from the R compartment, unlike in, say, the SIR model from Figure 4.4, where people can only depart the S compartment. This means the S population may decrease less quickly, or can remain at some nonzero population value, or can increase. These possibilities mean we may not run out of Susceptibles as a way of ending the illness, so it is possible for the Infectious compartment to never reduce to 0 people.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. While we could still call the R compartment “Removed”, it may be more precise to say that R here means “Recovered”. This indicates that everyone in the R compartment has recovered from illness and is still alive, which is important when we say that people in the R compartment are able to move to the Susceptible compartment where they may be infected again.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. These differential equations go with the compartmental diagram in Figure 12.3:
    \begin{align*} \frac{dS}{dt} \amp = \alpha R - \beta S I\\ \frac{dI}{dt} \amp = \beta S I - \gamma I\\ \frac{dR}{dt} \amp = \gamma I - \alpha R\text{.} \end{align*}
